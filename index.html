Frequently asked questions

What is “Inside the Linux Kernel”?

A free, online book that explains how the Linux kernel works at the level of its data structures, algorithms, locking rules and design decisions. It targets Linux 7.0.12 and follows each mechanism through the source on both x86_64 and ARM64.

Which topics does it cover?

Memory addressing, processes, interrupts, synchronization (RCU, qspinlocks), the EEVDF scheduler, memory management, system calls, the VFS, block drivers, the page cache, ext4, IPC, networking, power management, eBPF, io_uring, containers and cgroups, Rust in the kernel, kernel security, KVM virtualization, tracing and debugging, plus boot and kernel modules.

How do I read it?

Open it in your browser — it is plain static HTML with no sign-up. Each chapter opens with its own table of contents, and cross-references between chapters are ordinary links. Use your browser’s search (Ctrl/⌘+F) inside a chapter.

Who is it for?

Systems programmers, driver and platform engineers, students of operating systems, and anyone who wants to read the Linux source with a map in hand.
